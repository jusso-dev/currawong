Reports are queued as ordinary outbound mail and are not DKIM signed: they are spooled as already deliverable with no tenant, so they skip the classify step where outbound signing happens, and signing needs a tenant key. Known limits: a `!0` size suffix parses the same as no limit; a `_report._dmarc` record's own `rua` override (RFC 9990 section 4 step 9) is not applied; rows that arrive after their day was reported (a message deferred past the one-hour grace period) are dropped; reports are per policy domain for the whole gateway, not per tenant.

## DLP (Australian identifiers)

The only identifier literals in the repository are published samples: the ATO's TFN (`123 456 782`) and ABN (`51 824 753 556`), ASIC's ACN examples, the common Medicare sample (`2123 45670 1`) and card scheme test numbers. Where tests need more distinct valid values they derive them at run time (`synthetic_tfns` in `crates/dlp/tests/detectors.rs`, `sample_tfns` in `crates/currawongd/tests/pipeline.rs`) and do not commit them.

- Single-digit changes. TFN (nine and eight digit), ABN and card (Luhn) checks reject every single-digit change. The Medicare check digit covers only the first eight digits, so changes to the issue number or IRN are not detectable. ASIC's ACN check uses even weights, so a change of 5 at weights 8, 6, 4 or 2 gives another valid ACN. `crates/dlp/tests/detectors.rs` asserts exactly that.
- False positives. One random nine-digit number in eleven is a valid TFN, so a bare number scores below the default `min_confidence`. It counts when "TFN" is within 60 bytes before or 30 after (+30), or anywhere in the same text, such as a column header (+15). Medicare works the same way. Eight-digit TFNs need the word nearby. Numbers glued to letters (`INV123456782`) lose 20.
- Evasion. Unicode decimal digits (fullwidth, Arabic-Indic and other common scripts) are read as digits, zero-width and bidi format characters are ignored, and up to three separators (spaces, tabs, line breaks, `. , - / _` and Unicode spaces and dashes) may sit between digit groups. Letters, other punctuation and wider gaps split numbers. The licence detector reads ASCII only.
- Work is linear in the text. One MiB of hostile input scans in a few hundred milliseconds in release; `hostile_input_scans_in_linear_time` bounds it at 1 s (2 s in dev builds, where the crate is built at opt-level 2). Text over 2 MiB is not scanned and marks the report incomplete.
- Driver's licences have no check digit. They are matched only next to a context word, and the state formats overlap, so the kind is "driver's licence", not a specific state. Samples are fully masked.
- Text extraction covers the subject, attachment names, text and HTML bodies (tags removed; comment, script, style and attribute text kept; numeric entities decoded), forwarded messages, and top-level docx (body, headers, footers, footnotes, endnotes, comments), xlsx (cells streamed, at most one million) and PDF attachments, using documents built in `crates/parser-worker/tests/text.rs`. Bodies are collected first. PDF and Office parsing only runs in the parser worker; in-process parsing, and the scanner-disabled path, mark documents as unread.
- Incomplete scans are held for review: text over the 1 MiB extraction budget, a document over 32 MiB or one that fails to parse (encrypted PDFs included), or documents skipped outside the worker. Documents inside zip or other archives are not text-extracted and do not mark the scan incomplete. Real-world Office and PDF files from other producers were not tested; scanned (image-only) PDFs yield no text.
- PDF extraction has no time or output bound of its own. The worker's CPU, memory and wall-clock limits apply. A hostile `sharedStrings.xml` count can make calamine over-allocate; that also only happens inside the worker.
- `cargo deny check` with `pdf-extract` and `calamine` added: bans, licences and sources pass. Advisories fail on new 2026 entries against `rustls` 0.23.45 and `zip` 2.4.2 and 8.6.0, which were already in the lockfile (`zip` 8.6.0 through `mail-auth`; `calamine` shares it). The `zip` advisories concern `extract()` to a directory, which neither the parser nor `calamine` calls. Upgrading those crates is left to a separate change.
- The decision is policy. DLP exposes `dlp.<kind>` counts and a `dlp.incomplete` flag as signals; the default pack (`config/policies/default.toml`) quarantines outbound mail in the `dlp` queue at 5 distinct TFNs, Medicare, card or licence numbers, or when the scan is incomplete. `crates/policy/tests/engine.rs` and `crates/currawongd/tests/pipeline.rs` cover it end to end.

## YARA

`cargo test -p currawong-scan yara` covers the starter rules on their fixtures, a broken rule file (logged, previous rules stay active), broken rules at startup (an error, not an empty set), an emptied directory (rejected), rule files in any case and in subdirectories, a dangling entry (an error, not skipped), misspelt action tags (rejected), compiler warnings (logged and printed by `--check-config`), the per-message budget, de-duplication and part-name cleaning, concurrent reloads, hot reload (`notify` `PollWatcher`, two-second interval, so it also works on bind mounts and network volumes), and matching of MIME parts and archive members both in-process and through a live parser worker, including a second rules generation sent to the same worker. `cargo test -p currawong-parser-worker yara` drives the real worker binary over stdin: rules load, generation check, and a bad blob dropping the old rules. `cargo test -p currawongd yara` covers the pipeline report in-process and through the worker with a spooled body, fail-closed when the rules cannot load, and `--check-config` rejecting a broken rule. The worker-backed tests in `currawong-scan` and `currawongd` need `target/debug/currawong-parser-worker`: `cargo test --workspace` builds it; on their own run `cargo build -p currawong-parser-worker` first. They fail rather than skip when it is missing or stale.

Matching runs in the parser worker (ADR 0002) because yara-x modules such as `pe`, `dotnet`, `elf`, `macho` and `lnk` parse hostile files. The supervisor compiles the rules and sends the serialised set to each worker as a `LoadRules` frame on its stdin, once per worker and rules generation, before the next job. No rules file exists on disk and the worker never reads rules from a path or compiles rule source. Parse requests name the generation; a worker without it fails the job closed. yara-x reserves 16 MiB of address space per scanner, inside the worker's 512 MiB `RLIMIT_AS`.

Loading is the slow step: yara-x 1.21 is built without `native-code-serialization`, so deserialising recompiles the rules' wasm with cranelift. The load has its own timeout (`scanner.yara.load_timeout_secs`, default 120 s), separate from `job_timeout_secs`. Measured on this machine (Apple silicon, load average 25 to 50 from parallel builds) with a release build of 5,000 generated rules, each with a text and a hex pattern (`cargo test --release -p currawong-scan --test yara -- --ignored --nocapture`): compile 1.1 s, serialised blob 1.3 MB as base64, worker load (deserialise and recompile) 1.1 s, a 10 MiB scan 0.1 s. Debug builds are several times slower. Real community sets with heavy regular expressions or modules will differ; the default 120 s leaves about a hundredfold margin over this measurement. Until the #23 change lands per-job CPU limits, the load's CPU time counts against the worker's lifetime `RLIMIT_CPU` (`worker_cpu_secs`); raise that for large rule sets.

Per message, matching has a budget of 20 s, 512 MiB scanned and 2,048 distinct parts; identical content is matched once. Exceeding the budget fails the job closed with the reason. yara-x module parsing (`pe`, `dotnet` and so on) cannot be interrupted by the yara-x timeout; the worker's job timeout and kill cover that case. Attachment names in matches have control characters removed and are capped at 128 characters.

Build cost: yara-x brings wasmtime and cranelift, about 145 extra crates. A cold debug build of yara-x and its dependencies took 10 min 11 s wall clock here, with yara-x itself about 6 min 45 s, on a machine with a load average near 50 from parallel builds, so expect less on an idle machine. Incremental builds are unaffected. Licences: yara-x is BSD-3-Clause; wasmtime and cranelift are `Apache-2.0 WITH LLVM-exception` and notify is `CC0-1.0`, both added to `deny.toml`.

Advisories: yara-x 1.21 (the latest) pins wasmtime ^45, which has seven RustSec advisories. Fixes exist only in other wasmtime lines (46.0.x and 47.x for two of them, all seven by 48.0.6 or 49.0.3), none semver-compatible with 45. They concern the component model, async, WASI, multiple engines and hostile wasm modules. yara-x builds wasmtime with only `cranelift`, `runtime` and `std` and uses one engine. The only wasm it runs is the rules blob, which currawongd compiles from operator rule files and hands to the worker over its stdin pipe, never from a path or from mail; RUSTSEC-2026-0359 (hostile wasm) therefore rests on the integrity of the rules directory and the supervisor. They are ignored in `deny.toml` with these reasons. Remove the ignores when yara-x moves to a fixed wasmtime. Separately, `cargo deny check advisories` currently fails on advisories that were already in the tree before YARA: rustls 0.23.45 (RUSTSEC-2026-0346 to 0349, fixed in 0.23.46) and zip 2.4.2 and 8.6.0 (RUSTSEC-2026-0367 to 0369, fixed in 9.0.0; 8.6.0 comes in through mail-auth). Those need their own change.

## Policy engine (#37)

`cargo test -p currawong-policy` and `cargo test -p currawongd --test policy` cover the engine. The corpus test runs every sample in `testdata/corpus/expected.toml` through the real pipeline and default pack, with an in-process fake clamd for EICAR.

- A built-in floor runs before any pack: malware and failed scans are quarantined, scans a required engine could not complete are deferred. Tenant policies cannot lift it. A platform global policy can lift named rules (`override_floor = ["malware"]`) for the mail its conditions match; each rule is lifted separately, and every such policy is logged as a warning when loaded.
- Storage is SQLite through `rusqlite`, not `sqlx`. The workspace already links `rusqlite` with bundled SQLite, and `sqlx-sqlite` would link a second `libsqlite3-sys`, which Cargo refuses. Migrations are plain SQL in `crates/policy/migrations/`, tracked with `PRAGMA user_version`. PostgreSQL is not supported yet. Store calls are synchronous; the admin API should call them through `spawn_blocking`.
- "Editable via API" is the `PolicyStore` Rust API (upsert, delete, groups, TOML import and export), namespaced per tenant. There is no HTTP endpoint or RBAC yet; that belongs to the admin issue.
- Tenant and direction are resolved per recipient from `relay.tenants` and `domains.accept`. Outbound user and group scopes match only an authenticated identity, and the SMTP session does not put the login on the envelope yet, so those scopes do not match outbound mail today. Binding MAIL FROM to the credential is left to the SMTP session owner.
- Header, redirect and bcc actions are applied before delivery. Redirect and bcc only reach the recipient tenant's domains or `policy.forward_domains`. Tenant redirect and bcc stop once the message carries three `Received` fields this gateway wrote; platform and mandatory ones always apply. A sender can forge our `Received` line to stop tenant forwarding, but not platform or mandatory forwarding. Banner is refused at load time until delivery can carry it out. `require_tls`, `require_dane`, `encrypt`, `encrypt_smime` and `encrypt_portal` load and are carried out (see below).
- Reject sends a DSN with the policy's code and a generic reason, only to authenticated senders or senders that passed SPF or DMARC, never to automatic mail, and at most once per message. Otherwise the message is ended without a bounce. Discard ends the message without quarantining it. Quarantine records the policy's queue on the quarantine index row (`policy` when none is named) and in the reason.
- The default pack tags spam with an `X-Currawong-Spam: [SPAM]` header, not a subject rewrite, because the spool does not rewrite bodies.
- Nothing produces a `spam` score yet, so the spam policies do not fire on real mail. The tests inject scores.
- When recipients (or tenants) get different outcomes the message is split. The plan is saved under `spool/split/` before any copy is made, and a retry finishes that plan rather than deciding again. Copies carry the same decision and get ids derived from the original and their recipients.
- Policies are checked on every pipeline pass, on a blocking thread, and reloaded only when the pack file's time or size or the database revision changes. A database is seeded from the pack once; after that the pack file is not read. A database with the layout of an earlier development build is refused with a clear error.

## Policy TLS and encryption (#35)

- `require_tls` and `require_dane` set `Envelope.tls` (`TlsRequirement`). Delivery applies the stronger of that and its own DANE, MTA-STS and `delivery.require_tls` rules: `require_tls` turns off every fallback to clear text, including MTA-STS `testing`; `require_dane` defers any host without a DANE plan. Tested with in-process STARTTLS sinks in `cargo test -p currawong-smtp --test outbound_tls policy_` (no STARTTLS defers with "policy require_tls for <domain> requires TLS with <host>: STARTTLS not offered"). No real MX was used.
- S/MIME uses the pure Rust `cms` crate (AES-256-CBC content, RSA PKCS#1 v1.5 key transport, issuer and serial recipient ids) instead of OpenSSL's `Pkcs7::encrypt`, so the gateway does not link a system OpenSSL (this machine has OpenSSL 4, which the `openssl` crate does not yet target). It sits behind the `smime` feature of `currawong-dlp`, on by default. Only RSA recipient certificates are used; they are read from `encrypt.smime_certs_dir/<address>.pem` and checked for their validity period only, not chain, revocation or key usage. The round trip is proven by decrypting in the test with `rsa` and `cbc`; no mail client (Outlook, Apple Mail, Thunderbird) opened the output. The S/MIME copy is DKIM signed afresh; on inbound mail it does not carry our Authentication-Results or ARC set.
- The portal store (`currawong_dlp::encrypt::portal`) and its routes (`currawong_admin::routes::portal`) are tested in-process (`cargo test -p currawong-dlp encrypt`, `cargo test -p currawong-admin portal`). The link and code mails are spooled for normal delivery and were not seen in a real mailbox. The code mail keeps the held message's tenant and TLS requirement and is never sent with less than `require_tls`, so the link and the code cannot both be read off one clear-text hop; a recipient whose MX offers no verified STARTTLS cannot get a code. Both POSTs are rate limited per client and per message; the client is the peer address, or the nearest untrusted `X-Forwarded-For` entry when the peer is in `encrypt.portal.trusted_proxies`. A locked message stays locked; `PortalStore::unlock` exists but no admin route or command calls it yet. Held messages are deleted after `retention_days` by an hourly sweep.
- Encryption runs after the policy decision and before delivery. A message a policy asked to encrypt is quarantined when it cannot be encrypted (no certificate for `encrypt_smime`, no portal for `encrypt_portal` or `encrypt`). PGP is out of scope.
- With URL rewriting (#29) on, the S/MIME form and the portal copy are made from the body with rewritten links, as delivered mail would carry; a message that cannot be encrypted is quarantined as it arrived. Not covered by a test.
- Mail that cannot be encrypted is held in the quarantine index (#38) under the policy's queue (`policy` when none is named). Known gap: the index does not keep `Envelope.tls`, and a release skips policy, so an administrator who releases such a message (or any held message a policy marked `require_tls`) sends it without encryption and with only the gateway's own TLS rules.

## Quarantine (#38)

`cargo test -p currawong-quarantine` and `cargo test -p currawongd --test quarantine` cover the store, digests and release.

- Index: `quarantine.sqlite` (rusqlite, migrations tracked with `PRAGMA user_version`), one row per held recipient with tenant, queue, sender, subject, reasons, expiry and release or delete state. Message bytes are one blob per spool message: files with mode 0600 under `blobs/` (directory 0700), or an S3-compatible bucket through `object_store`. Holding again after a crash adds nothing (row ids derive from message and recipient).
- Queue names are canonical: trimmed, lower case, `_` as `-` (so `Virus` and `scan_failed` are `virus` and `scan-failed`), at hold time and in config. `malware` is the floor's name for the issue's `virus` queue. A message held after `scanner.max_scan_deferrals` goes to `scan-failed`, like a failed scan; a quarantine action with no queue goes to `policy`.
- Search: `list` scopes by role and tenant in SQL, filters by queue, sender, recipient, subject substring and date range, and pages with a `created_at:id` cursor (100 per page by default, 500 at most). The spam score is stored when the classifier reported one.
- Release re-injects the original bytes into the spool for that one recipient with `pipeline = "release"` (or `"release-ham"` for the spam queue), under an id derived from the item. The audit trail gets `release_started` (or `delete_started`) before anything changes and `release` (or `delete`) once it is done, so a failed attempt shows as a start without a finish; the item stays held and a retry finishes the same release. Expired items cannot be released or deleted. The bytes are deleted once no recipient still has the message held; a failed blob delete is left to the purge. The scheduler delivers it without running policy, so that release cannot be quarantined again; inbound mail keeps the Authentication-Results it was held with and outbound mail is DKIM signed at that point. Every release, delete, purge and denied attempt goes to the audit sink: an `AuditSink` trait for the admin crate (#39), and until then the `quarantine_audit` table, which triggers make append-only.
- Roles: auditors read only; end users see and act on their own inbound items in `quarantine.user_release_queues` (default `spam`) only, never on outbound holds, whose recipient is the outside party the mail was held from; tenant and MSP admins act within their tenant; platform admins act anywhere. Malware, virus and PSPF items need an administrator and a reason, and cannot be configured as user queues. MSP child tenants are not resolved yet; #39 maps its sessions to a `Principal`.
- Digests: one message per recipient for newly held inbound items in user queues, and one link-free notice per internal sender for held outbound mail (an administrator releases it), at most every `digest_interval_hours`, queued as ordinary mail with `Auto-Submitted: auto-generated`. Digests skip the scan pipeline: they are generated here and hold only escaped index fields. Links are HMAC-SHA256 over item, recipient, action and expiry (verified in constant time with `hmac`'s `verify_slice`), expire after `link_ttl_hours` or at the item's expiry, and work once. `GET` on a link only shows a confirmation form, so a link scanner cannot release mail; the `POST` acts. Link pages send `no-store`, `no-referrer` and a CSP that forbids framing.
- Each digest or notice lists at most 100 items and says how many more are waiting; those go in the next run. Outbound holds from the null sender get no notice. A digest that fails to queue is logged and retried next run without stopping the others. `digests = true` needs `quarantine.listen`. A stored `direction` other than `inbound` is read as outbound, which end users cannot act on. `list` shows only held, unexpired items unless `all` is set.
- The HTTP endpoints are an axum router in `crates/quarantine` (`/quarantine/link/{token}`, `/quarantine/items`, `/quarantine/items/{id}/release`, `/quarantine/items/{id}/delete`). `quarantine.listen` serves it from the daemon; admin routes answer 401 until #39 mounts the router behind its authentication.
- Retention: `retention_days` (default 30) with per-queue overrides. The daemon purges hourly. Per item: audit, blob, row, under the action lock so a release in progress keeps its bytes. A failing item is logged and retried next pass; the rest still go.
- The digest-link key file is refused when group or others can read it. A `quarantine.listen` address off loopback is logged as a warning: the endpoints are plain HTTP behind a TLS proxy.
- One action lock serialises releases, deletes and purge steps for the whole store. That is simple and correct for one node; a slow S3 call holds up other releases meanwhile.
- `hmac` is 0.12 because 0.13 needs the digest 0.11 line and the workspace `sha2` is 0.10.

Not run, and why:

- A real S3 bucket. The S3 store is tested with `object_store`'s in-memory backend and the residency refusal of a `us-east-1` endpoint. Requests go through a connector that checks and pins every request with the residency guard and never follows a redirect (so an S3 307 to another region fails), with certificate verification from the platform trust store. That connector was not exercised against a live endpoint or a redirecting server.
- A real browser and mail client. The digest HTML and the link pages were checked in tests for escaping and form behaviour, not rendered in Outlook, Gmail or a browser. Digest delivery to the user goes to the configured route or MX like any mail and was tested against an in-process SMTP sink only.
- Quarantine items written by earlier builds (`<id>/message.eml` and `reason.txt` under the quarantine directory) are not migrated into the index. Startup logs a warning with their count.
- Bayes training on release is keyed by the released message's id, so each recipient's spam release trains once. The daemon test runs without a classifier, so `learn` itself is covered only by the classify crate's tests.
- Admin HTTP routes answer 401 in the daemon until #39 sets the caller; admin release is reachable through the library API only.
- Released mail is delivered as it was held: the release skips policy, so its links are not rewritten by `url_rewrite` (#29).

## URL rewriting (#29)

`cargo test -p currawong-scan urls`, `cargo test -p currawong-admin` and `cargo test -p currawongd --test urls` cover it.

- Round trip: every http(s) link in `testdata/urls/links.eml` (a quoted-printable UTF-8 text part with a link split by a soft line break, a base64 ISO-8859-1 HTML part with `&amp;`, an upper-case scheme and an `<area>`, and a 7bit text part) is rewritten, and each token verifies back to the link a browser would open, with the message id, recipients and arrival time. The admin test clicks every rewritten link through the click service over a socket and checks each 302 `Location`. Allow-listed links, `mailto:`, the S/MIME signed part and the HTML attachment are unchanged; headers, the preamble, other parts, charsets and transfer encodings are byte for byte or equivalent, and LF-only messages keep LF line endings.
- Link finding follows the browser: WHATWG URL parsing (tabs, newlines, backslashes, scheme case), numeric character references with or without `;` (`&#104ttp`, `&#x3a`), the legacy `&amp` rule, protocol-relative `//host` links (taken as https), relative links under the first `<base href>` wherever it appears (found in a first pass), `href`, `xlink:href` and `formaction` on any element (VML buttons such as `<v:roundrect href>` included; `<link>` style sheets and `<base>` excluded), `<form action>` and `text/x-amp-html` parts. A relative link with no base to resolve it (other than a `#fragment`) counts as skipped.
- Tampering: a changed payload, a changed or cut signature, a token without a signature, junk, a token from another key and an oversized token all get HTTP 400; an expired token gets 410. The MAC is checked with `hmac`'s constant-time comparison before the payload is decoded.
- Blocked after delivery: a link is delivered and clicked (302), its domain is then added to the block list file, and the same link gets the 403 block page. The click log records both. `currawongd --test urls` runs this through `classify_pending` with the config-built rewriter and click service, and checks that the delivered spool body carries the rewritten link, that the ARC seal over the rewritten body validates in `mail-auth`, and that outbound mail is not rewritten.
- Authentication results: Authentication-Results and the ARC set's ARC-Authentication-Results come from the message as it arrived, and only the ARC-Message-Signature and ARC-Seal are computed over the rewritten copy. A DKIM-signed message whose DMARC pass rests on DKIM alone shows `dkim=pass` and `dmarc=pass` in both after rewriting.
- Signed parts: only a `multipart/signed` whose `protocol` is a detached S/MIME or PGP signature, whose second part has that type, and whose signature parses (a DER or BER CMS `ContentInfo` with content type signedData, or an OpenPGP signature packet, armoured or binary) is left alone. For clear-signed PGP text only the span from the header to a signature block that parses is left alone; links before or after it are rewritten. Signatures are not verified, so a well-formed signature from anyone still exempts its part; the policy below covers that. A bare, mismatched or junk-signature wrapper is rewritten.
- Links that are not rewritten (signed parts with links, a charset that is not ASCII-compatible such as UTF-16 or ISO-2022-JP, undecodable parts, HTML the rewriter gave up on, links over 8 KiB, relative links with no base, and whole messages over `max_message_bytes`, over 10,000 links, nested past 512 multiparts or 32 attached messages, or unparsable) set the `url_rewrite.skipped` policy flag with a note. Rewriting runs before the policy decision for that reason. The default and government packs: quarantine (`url-rewrite` queue) when DMARC failed; otherwise tag `X-Currawong-Links: not-rewritten`, and add `X-Currawong-Warning: links from an unauthenticated sender were not checked` when DMARC did not pass. Mail with no DMARC record is too common (and large messages too ordinary) to hold on this flag alone; a pack can quarantine every skipped message instead.
- Only copies that are delivered carry rewritten links. Quarantine, bounces and split copies with another verdict keep the original. Tokens expire `ttl_days` (default 7) after the message arrived, so a retry of a split plan rebuilds the same bytes the saved ARC set covers.
- Crash safety: before the rewritten body replaces the spool body, the arrival bytes are written to `spool/rewrite/<id>.eml` (fsynced file and directory); the body rename is followed by a directory fsync, then the envelope update, then the copy is removed. A message still pending with that copy is restored and classified from the original. The test simulates the crash state; power loss was not exercised (see the spool note above). A crash after the envelope update but before the copy is removed leaves a stray file in `spool/rewrite/`. Split copies are written marked `split`, which neither classification nor delivery picks up; the original's retry acts on any copy still marked so from the saved plan, and rebuilds the rewritten body from all the plan's recipients (sorted), so it matches the saved ARC set. Tested by leaving a `split` copy behind after an interrupted plan.
- Click service: `HEAD` is answered without checking or recording. Rate limits count verified clicks only, so junk tokens cannot use up anyone's allowance: `clicks_per_minute_per_client` (default 600) keyed on the client address (an IPv6 /64 is one client), and `clicks_per_minute_per_link` (default 30) per recipient listed in the token (50 recipients assumed when the list was too long to include), then 429. The client is the TCP peer, or, when the peer is in `trusted_proxies`, the nearest address in `X-Forwarded-For` that is not a trusted proxy; the header is ignored from anyone else. Warning page for `warn` list entries, or for every site without an `allow` entry when `warn_unknown` is set, with a continue link; a block wins over a continue. Pages send `frame-ancestors 'none'`. Each limiter forgets idle keys past 50,000 and starts again if they are all live, so a flood from many addresses costs bounded memory and time. Clicks are recorded in a 0600 JSON lines log rotated at `click_log_max_bytes`, and emitted as `url_click` events on the event bus (the event itself is not asserted in a test; the exporters' `url_click` handling is tested in `currawong-integrations`).
- Bounds: a byte scan refuses messages nested past 512 multiparts or 32 attached messages before `mail-parser` sees them (it recurses, and a stack overflow would abort the daemon; the same limits as the classify stage). Messages just under the limits parse on a 2 MiB stack, the size of a tokio blocking thread, in the tests. lol_html runs non-strict with a 32 MiB memory cap. Rewriting runs on a blocking thread, so a panic is a note and a skipped flag, not a crash. Hostile and random inputs are in the tests and in the `url_rewrite` fuzz target. A 7bit or 8bit part whose line would pass 998 octets after rewriting is re-encoded as quoted-printable and its `Content-Transfer-Encoding` header replaced.

Known limits, not done:

- Rewriting parses MIME with `mail-parser` in the supervisor, not in the parser worker (ADR 0002), behind the nesting scan and size cap, on a blocking thread. Moving it into the worker needs the rewritten body in the worker reply.
- Bare URLs in HTML text (which some clients turn into links), links in attached messages and HTML attachments are not rewritten. UTF-16 and ISO-2022-JP parts are flagged, not transcoded.
- Links read from QR codes (#27) are in images, so they cannot be rewritten and get no click-time check. The scan report notes how many; they do not set `url_rewrite.skipped` (QR codes are common in legitimate mail), and a pack that wants them held can use the `qr_url` flag. Tested with `urls_qr_code_links_are_noted_not_rewritten`.
- One body serves every recipient of a copy, so the token's recipient field lists them all (left empty past 256 bytes), and it can be read by anyone holding the link.
- Reputation at click time is the local list only. The `UrlReputation` trait is where a threat-intel feed plugs in.
- The block list fails closed: after one good load, a missing file or a zero-byte file keeps the last good list (logged), so delete-and-recreate or a half-written file never unblocks everything. A file with only comments clears it. Entries that are neither a domain nor an http(s) URL (`evil.test:8080`, `evil.test/path`) are logged and ignored; `*.domain` means the domain and its subdomains.
- Full URLs, including query strings, go to the click log and the event bus.
- A crash after the envelope update but before the saved original is removed leaves `spool/rewrite/<id>.eml`; nothing sweeps it yet.
- Link scanners that prefetch with `GET` (for example mailbox-side safe-links services) are counted as clicks.
- Without `trusted_proxies` set, the per-client limit sees the TLS proxy's address for every click and becomes one shared limit; set it to the proxy's address in production.
- The click service speaks plain HTTP and expects a TLS proxy in front of `base_url`. No TLS proxy, real browser or real mail client was exercised; the tests use raw HTTP/1.1 over a local socket.

## HTML smuggling and QR codes (#27)

`cargo test -p currawong-parser-worker --test qr --test html_smuggling` and `cargo test -p currawongd --test pipeline qr_urls` cover it. QR fixtures are generated in the tests with the `qrcode` crate and the smuggling "payload" is base64 of repeated harmless text; no fixture files are committed.

- QR codes are read with `image` and `rqrr` from every image part and archive member (by content, not by declared type), 8 codes per image. Transparent backgrounds are flattened onto white, and an image with no code is tried again inverted (light on dark). Images over 8 MP are scaled down before detection. Decoded `http(s)://` and `www.` links join `urls` (so classify's lookalike and spam checks see them) and are listed with source `qr` in `url_sources`, which is carried onto the scan report; the part gets the `QrCode` flag. Policy sees `qr_code`, `qr_url` and the count `qr_urls`. The default pack adds `X-Currawong-QR-Code: present` and delivers, since QR codes are common in legitimate mail. Image decoding runs in the in-process fallback as well as the worker.
- QR limits. An image over 25 MP, or one whose decode needs more than 128 MiB, is a possible decode bomb: it is not read and gets `LimitExceeded`, which the pipeline quarantines like an archive limit. Images past 64 images, 200 MP or 10 s of QR work in one message are not read either, but because QR is only a tagging signal they get `QrSkipped` (policy flag `qr.skipped`) and the message is not held; the default pack ignores that flag, so a QR code in an image past the budget goes unread. Images under 21 pixels a side cannot hold a code and are neither read nor counted. A panic inside the QR detector is caught and treated as no code; no test provokes one. Inline `data:` images inside HTML, bare domains without `www.`, and codes that need more rotation or perspective correction than `rqrr` does are not read.
- Known gap: QR codes in PDFs. Rendering the first 3 pages needs the parser worker built with `--features pdfium` (off by default, so the shipped worker and the Debian package do not read PDF QR codes) and the Pdfium shared library where `Pdfium::bind_to_system_library` looks. The worker binds it at start-up, before its sandbox is applied, and renders in the worker only, at about 150 dpi, within the QR budget above (a single page render cannot be interrupted; the worker job timeout covers that). Not run, and why: no Pdfium library is installed on this machine, so the feature was compiled and linted (`cargo clippy -p currawong-parser-worker --features pdfium --all-targets`) but never rendered a page, and there is no test for it. When the library is missing a warning is logged once and PDFs are not checked; nothing marks that on the report. `pdfium-render` is in `Cargo.lock` because Cargo locks optional dependencies too; `cargo deny check` does not cover it by default (`--features currawong-parser-worker/pdfium` does, and passed bans, licences and sources).
- HTML smuggling is a static score from 0 to 1 (integer points out of 20) over HTML parts and archive members: `text/html`, a `.html`, `.htm`, `.xhtml`, `.xht` or `.shtml` name, or a part with no better type whose first KiB has `<html`, `<script` or `<!doctype html`. Base64 counts as a payload when it adds up past 50 KiB (0.4) or 8 KiB (0.2): inside `<script>` every chunk of 32 or more characters counts, so `"AAA" + "BBB"` and chunk arrays add up and a `data:image/` URI there is not exempt; outside scripts only runs of 1,024 or more count and an inline image (`src=`, `srcset=` or `url(` followed by `data:image/...;base64,`) is exempt. `new Blob(`, `atob(`, `createObjectURL(`, `msSaveBlob`/`msSaveOrOpenBlob` and a download trigger (`.download =`, `setAttribute("download", ...)`) add 0.15 each, with whitespace and quoted property access (`window["atob"]`) allowed; a Blob with an object URL or save call adds 0.25 more. At 0.7 the part gets `HtmlSmuggling` (policy flag `html_smuggling`) and the default pack quarantines it in the `phishing` queue. So a Blob, an object URL and one more call or a small payload is held, which will also hold legitimate HTML reports with a "download" button. JavaScript is not run, so string-built names (`window["at" + "ob"]`), payloads fetched at run time, and payloads hidden in non-base64 encodings are missed. SVG is not scored. The false-positive rate on real mail is not measured.

## ADR 0002 and the worker transport

ADR 0002 says the supervisor talks to parser workers over a Unix socket. Issue #23 specifies length-prefixed JSON on stdin and stdout. M1 follows the issue: `LengthDelimitedCodec` on the worker's standard streams. The ADR decision is unchanged. The transport should be reconciled in a later ADR update rather than silently rewritten.

## Malware accuracy

Official ClamAV signatures are the baseline only. See `docs/adr/0005-malware-engines.md`. Do not treat a clean clamd result, on the official set alone, as proof that a message is safe. File-type and macro findings are part of the M1 decision.

## PSPF protective markings (#36)

- The parser follows the EPMS header syntax as published for 2018.6 and 2024.1 (taken from Microsoft's Australian Government Purview guidance, which quotes the standard). The EPMS 2026 text on protectivesecurity.gov.au was not retrieved, so any 2026 changes are not reflected, and the test examples are built from that syntax rather than copied from the standard. The CAVEAT form, the three ACCESS markers and the EXPIRES/DOWNTO pairing should be checked against the 2026 text.
- The parser is hand written rather than built on `nom`, to avoid a new dependency; it is covered by the `pspf` unit tests and the DLP fuzz entry (`fuzz_scan` now also drives `pspf::parse_subject` and `pspf::inspect`).
- PROTECTED "only over TLS" is not enforced by the government pack yet: it needs the `require_tls` action (#35).
- Markings are not yet copied into exported SIEM events; the verdict event carries the policy id (`pspf-*`).

## BEC detection (#31)

- Run with `cargo test -p currawong-classify bec` (the issue's `-p currawong-scan` command names an old location).
- Heuristics are tuned on synthetic fixtures only; false-positive rates on real mail are not measured.
- The `prepend_banner` policy action is not implemented yet: policy banner actions need message-body rewriting (being added with URL rewriting, #29). Until then the default pack marks sub-threshold impersonation with an `X-Currawong-Warning` header (`bec-warn`) that transport rules or clients can turn into a banner.
- A message to recipients in several tenants is scored once, for the first recipient's tenant.
- The sender history only records From addresses of delivered inbound mail that passed SPF or DMARC; it keeps at most 200,000 pairs per tenant and forgets senders not seen for a year.
